# Of Weight and Sinking

*Why a ship that carries too much does not carry it far.*

## Everything weighs something

The ship floats because the water she pushes aside weighs as much as she does. Whatever is put aboard makes her heavier, sits her lower in the water, and makes her slower. That is true of cargo and of men, of the provisions and of the guns.

The **cannon** are the heaviest things aboard that are not cargo. A ship that sends her guns ashore sits higher and sails faster. A master who has no wish to fight may think this a good bargain. One who meets a pirate will not.

## The waterline

Look at the rails. If the water is a long way below them, all is well. As the ship is loaded, the sea comes nearer. If the **waterline stays above the rails** for more than a few moments, the sea comes aboard faster than it can run out, and the ship is lost.

- Do not load a ship until her rails are almost awash and then take her out into a heavy sea.
- A load that would sink her in a swell may be carried safely in a calm. Weigh the weather as well as the cargo.
- Cargo too heavy to carry by hand is loaded by the **harbour crane**. Look to the weight before it is swung aboard, not after.

## Leaks

A ship that strikes a rock, or is struck by shot, will let in water. The water collects in the hold and adds its own weight to the ship, and a heavier ship sits lower still.

- The **pumps** drive water out of the hold. Take hold of the **pump handle** (it is outlined when you look at it) and keep your hold: the handle works up and down under your hands, and the pumps run for as long as you hold on. Let go and they stop. They work slowly, and a hole that is large will beat them.
- A hole can be **plugged**, and this should be done before anything else.

## What to do

Know what your ship will bear. Keep the stores, the crew and the guns in proportion. Keep the pumps in order. If the rails are going under, throw the cargo over the side before the ship is lost, for the cargo can be bought again.
