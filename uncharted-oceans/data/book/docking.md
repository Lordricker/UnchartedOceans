# Of Docking and Mooring

*How a ship is brought to a pier without breaking her or it.*

## The berth

Each pier has berths marked out for the ships it can take, and each berth has a size. A small pier will not take a large ship. A berth shows itself as an **outline of light on the water** as you come near: a low curtain of light round the edge of the berth, and a tall shaft of light standing up out of the water at each corner. It is cut to the size of the ship that is meant to lie there.

## Coming in

1. **Slow down.** A ship that comes in too fast cannot be moored, and will strike the pier. The brig must be under about **two and a half knots** (four and a half kilometres in the hour) to be moored; count it with the log.
2. **Line her up** with the berth. The pier lies on the **port** side as you come in. The berth has a heading of its own, and the ship must lie close to it.
3. **Bring the whole hull inside the outline.** If the light is **amber**, she is not yet in, or is too fast, or is crooked. When she is in, slow enough and straight, it turns **green**.
4. **Give the order to put the lines ashore.** When the light is green, **E Dock** shows at the foot of your sight: press **E**. The ship is then drawn in against the fenders by the lines to the bollards, and held. After a moment the **gangway** is run out to the pier, on the port side between the two forward guns.

You cannot walk off the pier side until the gangway is out.

## Casting off

To **cast off**, take hold of the wheel: **E Cast off** then shows at the foot of your sight, and pressing **E** lets the lines go. The lines are let go and the ship drifts off the pier. You cannot cast off while a man is standing on the gangway.

## Dangers

- **Shoal water.** The keel reaches more than two metres below the water. If it touches the bottom the ship slows and is pushed back toward deeper water. The waves count, so a trough can ground you where a crest would not. Watch the water under the keel when you are near land.
- **The pier.** If the hull strikes it, the ship is stopped and thrown back, and the harder you hit, the worse it is.
- **The wind** will push a ship off her line as she nears the pier. Come in slowly enough to correct for it.
