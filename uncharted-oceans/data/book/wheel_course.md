# Of the Wheel and the Course

*How a ship is steered, and kept steering.*

## The wheel

The wheel turns the rudder, and the rudder turns the ship. Take hold of the rim at any spot and move your hand the way the rim moves under it. Turning the wheel to the right, as you stand behind it, puts the rudder over so that the ship turns to **starboard**. Turning it to the left sends her to **port**.

Hold the wheel and your eyes stay where they are. Let go and you may look about again.

## Way on

A rudder does nothing until water runs past it. A ship lying still, or nearly so, will not answer her wheel at all. She must have **way on**, and the faster she goes the quicker she turns. Even so she is slow to begin and slow to stop, so ease the wheel back before she has swung as far as you want.

## Amidships and holding a course

When the wheel is straight, the rudder is **amidships**. You can bring the wheel back to this without your hand and let her run.

You can also ask a steady hand to **hold the course** you are on. The wheel then moves of itself, a little at a time, as a good helmsman's would, to keep her head where it is. The course stays held even after you walk away, to haul a line or look at the chart. It is let go if you take the wheel yourself, or ask for it.

The words for these orders are shown at the foot of your sight whenever your hand is on the wheel.

## The compass

Before you hold a course, know it. The compass stands in the binnacle just ahead of the wheel, and a second one stands on the chart table in the cabin. The card inside it keeps its north, and the ship turns round it. The **red mark** on the rim is fixed to the ship and points at her bow, so the number under the mark is the way she is heading.

A bearing is given in degrees from north, going round with the sun: east is ninety, south one hundred and eighty, west two hundred and seventy.

## Cautions

- A ship under sail pulls to windward by herself (**weather helm**). Expect to hold a little wheel against it.
- Heavy weather and a heavy sea will carry her off her course. A held course is a good servant and a poor master. Look at the compass.
