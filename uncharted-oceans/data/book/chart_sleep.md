# Of the Chart and of Sleep

*How far there is to go, and how to cross it.*

## Reading the chart

The chart on the cabin table shows the whole world, about twelve hundred kilometres across and eight hundred from top to bottom. Land is green and brown, shallows are pale, and deep water is dark. Every port is marked with its name, and the one you are bound for has a **gold diamond**.

- **North** is at the top of the picture. The chart lies on the table with its top toward the bow, but the picture does not turn with the ship, so north stays at the top however she heads.
- The **grid** lines are fifty kilometres apart, and every second line is marked with its number. The **scale bar** in the corner is a hundred kilometres long. Lay it against the distance you want.
- Your ship is the small **yellow dot**. It does not show which way she heads: read that from the compass. A second compass stands on the chart table beside the chart.
- Round the dot is a **ring**. The longer you have been away from the last port, the **wider the ring** grows, for no master is ever quite sure where he is. Out of sight of land, trust the ring and not the point.
- There is no road drawn to the port. Lay a line from your dot to the diamond yourself, and see what lies across it.

## Knots and kilometres

The log gives your speed in **knots**. The chart is in **kilometres**. One knot is **1.852 kilometres in an hour**, so:

**kilometres per hour = knots × 1.852**

**distance in kilometres = knots × 1.852 × hours**

- 3 knots: 5.6 kilometres an hour
- 5 knots: 9.3 kilometres an hour
- 6 knots: 11.1 kilometres an hour
- 8 knots: 14.8 kilometres an hour
- 10 knots: 18.5 kilometres an hour

A ship making **6 knots** goes **11.1 km** in an hour, and **222 km** in twenty hours. Right across the chart, twelve hundred kilometres, is about **four days** at seven knots.

## How many hours?

Read the **distance** off the chart, take your **speed** from the log, and divide:

**hours = distance ÷ (knots × 1.852)**

A port **150 km** away, at **5 knots** (9.3 km an hour), is about **16 hours** off.

## Sleep

Click the bed. A window asks how many hours. Type a number and press Enter, or press the **Sleep until morning** button to sleep to sunrise, or press Esc to get up again.

While you sleep the ship **keeps the speed** she had when you lay down, and **goes on turning as the wheel is set**.

- With the wheel **amidships** (press **Q** at the wheel) or the course **held** (**Z**), she runs straight on the heading you lay down on.
- With the wheel **left over**, she sails a great curve. At about 5 knots or more, the wheel hard over turns her **4 degrees every second**: a full circle in a minute and a half. The less the wheel is over, the slower she comes round, in proportion: a wheel half over takes twice as long. Under about 5 knots the rudder bites less, and she turns more slowly still.
- Her speed does not change, so the **width of the circle** is her speed divided by how fast she turns. Work it out and you can lay a long curve in your sleep, round a headland, or up toward a wind that a straight course could not take you.
- A wheel a **very little** off centre still makes a circle, and over many hours a large one. The few degrees either side of amidships count as straight.

- Before you sleep, **hold your course** and be sure the sails are set and drawing. A ship lying idle, or turned the wrong way, will carry you nowhere or somewhere you do not want to be.
- You **wake early** when a port is close, at the edge of the chart, or when a storm is closing in. You cannot lie down at all while a storm is on you.
- **Land does not wake you.** If your course runs into a shore, the ship sails on until her keel touches the bottom and stays there. You wake aground with the shore under the bow, and you must work her off by hand.
- Then look at the chart again. The dot will have moved, and the ring will be wider.

## The mark on the horizon

The gold **diamond** on the horizon is the port you are bound for, and it points the way to it however far off. It gives no distance: take that off the chart. There may be land between you and it. The diamond does not go round the shore for you.

## The year

A year is **forty-eight days**, twelve to each season. The **Ship's Log** tells you today's date, the season, and when the next begins. The wind and the storms follow the seasons (see *Of Sails and the Wind*).
